# AI Satisficing - A New Dark Pattern

🗓️ November 30th, 2025

![Brain being put on pause](ai-satisficing-banner.webp)

The most dangerous thing about modern AI isn't what it can't do—it's what it does well enough. When ChatGPT returns a serviceable answer in seconds, why would anyone spend minutes refining their prompt? Why iterate when adequate is instant? This seductive logic is AI Satisficing—and it trains us to accept mediocrity as the new baseline.

## Why It Matters

- **Cognitive atrophy is accelerating**. When AI's first draft is "good enough," the critical thinking muscles stop exercising, no longer distinguishing excellent work from adequate work. Like a body that stops moving, a mind that stops pushing atrophies.
- **The competitive edge is eroding**. Everyone has access to the same AI tools. The differentiator isn't the model—it's the ability to extract exceptional results through rigorous prompting, iteration, and judgment. Satisficing eliminates that advantage.
- **"Good enough" becomes the ceiling, not the floor**. When accepting AI's baseline output as the final product, standards get lower. Excellence requires effort—and AI Satisficing simplifies the avoidance of that effort.

## The Backstory

[Sean Goedecke labelled AI Sycophancy as the first major dark pattern in Large Language Models](https://www.seangoedecke.com/ai-sycophancy). He observed that models are trained to flatter us, prioritizing agreement over objective truth.

While preparing the curriculum for my recent Prompt Engineering training, I realized we are facing a second, equally dangerous dark pattern.

[Stanford's Jeremy Utley](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yMOmmnjy3sE) calls it AI Satisficing. While Utley describes it as a cognitive bias, I see it as something more dangerous—a dark pattern. A gateway drug to dependence.

### What is AI Satisficing?

Herbert Simon originally coined "satisficing" to describe settling for an option that is "adequate" rather than optimizing for the best result. Jeremy Utley applies the concept of satisficing to AI and Large Language Models (LLMs).

But while Utley frames it as a cognitive bias, I see it as something more dangerous—a dark pattern.

When LLMs were primitive, we *had* to use advanced techniques—personas, rich context, clear constraints—just to get coherent answers.

With the latest LLMs instantly generating "good enough" answers, we stop iterating. Why refine what's already serviceable? Gradually, our prompts become lazier—the model compensates for our decreasing effort.

**This is the trap.** Because the output is "sufficient," we accept the model's first draft as the final product. We let the model's baseline become our ceiling.

### The Gateway Drug

I view AI Satisficing as a gateway drug to cognitive atrophy—and like any addiction, we don't notice the dependency until it's too late.

The pattern is seductive: each LLM response generates a dopamine hit of instant answers. Each "good enough" response reinforces the behavior. Why struggle when satisfaction is one click away?

But here's what at stake:

- **First, the habit of iteration**—we forget that first drafts are meant to be terrible
- **Then, the instinct to question**—why scrutinize what's already coherent?
- **Finally, the capacity for deep work**—that neural pathway atrophies from disuse

We've traded intellectual autonomy for convenience. The model becomes a crutch, then a wheelchair, then life support.

That's not assistance. That's dependence.

## The Takeaway

**Reject Sufficiency**. To avoid this dark pattern:

- **Recognize the Baseline**: Treat the LLM's first response as a rough draft, never a deliverable.
- **Maintain Rigor**: The better the model gets, the more valuable your context and constraints become.
- **Iterate**: Use the time saved by the model to refine the output, not to disconnect from the process.

AI should amplify your standards, not replace them.
